## 2026-10-20: Sammy v03 (the owner's new frames, a steel-grey chainsaw, the review fixes)

**What happened:** the owner generated the frames the v02 rebuild was missing (with the same image tool as the master sheets), and two reviews of v02 listed its rough spots. All 13 strips were re-cut in one go as `-v03.png` (the `-v02` and `-v01` files stay on disk unused; the loader takes the highest number). **Owner-generated frames now in the game:** three crawl in-betweens (run frames 2, 5 and 7, so the run is a forward 8-frame cycle instead of five poses played out and back), the head-down falling frame (fall frame 2, replacing the back-view stand-in), the settled belly-up play-dead pose (frame 4, the held one), the K.O. air and bounce frames (frames 2 and 3, replacing the duplicate tumble), and the full 8-frame chainsaw run (replacing one sheet frame repeated eight times). Built with the importer's character mode in two runs on one saved palette: the main map at scale 0.35 (10 strips) and the air map at 0.32 (jump, fall, land). Flags as v02: `--saturation-boost 1.15 --recolor "#480000>#6c2424:30"`, plus `--palette` with the 15-colour file. License rows: `design/ASSET_SOURCES.csv` (same stance as v02: owner-generated source art, heavily processed; the image tool's terms are still the owner's to confirm). Screenshots and a GIF per strip were checked at desktop and phone sizes.

**Proposed choices (not approved, all reversible):**
- **K.O. frames mirrored so the head stays on the right.** The owner's two K.O. frames came out facing up-left and down-left while the sheet's slides that follow have the head on the right. The mirrored copies keep the head on the right through the whole tumble (the tail changes side once); the unmirrored frames are in the prep notes if the owner prefers them.
- **Two steel greys in the palette, and the bar greyed before import.** The 14-colour v02 palette had no neutral grey, so the saw bar snapped to a fur highlight and read as a tan log. The palette is now 15 colours (the limit): the light mauve `#b49090` (0.4-2.6% of the non-chainsaw strips, mostly the bar itself in the chainsaw strips) made room for **`#b4b4b4` and `#6c6c6c`**. The bar is only slightly less saturated than the fur in the source (warm greys against warm browns), so a plain recolour would have greyed fur highlights in every strip; instead the low-saturation pixels inside the chainsaw frame boxes only (big connected patches, so face highlights are untouched) were pushed to a neutral, slightly cool grey on the prep sheet before import. The importer's area vote then smooths the 2 px chain teeth away, so a small deterministic pass, **`game/tools/chainsaw-teeth.mjs`**, paints them back just inside the bar's outline (a light tooth on every other column, dark between, and the dark chain under them; the 1 px outline itself stays `#242400`, so the outline rule holds on all 13 strips; a review of the first v03 cut found the teeth painted onto the outline and it was redone). On the run and rev strips the pattern shifts per frame so the chain runs; the idle gets no shift, so its four frames are identical. The exact commands, run from `game/` on the importer's output (the `--install` commands below write it straight to the `-v03.png` names): `node tools/chainsaw-teeth.mjs ../assets/characters/character-sammy/sammy-chainsaw-idle-strip-v03.png 64`, `... sammy-chainsaw-run-strip-v03.png 64 0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1`, `... sammy-chainsaw-rev-strip-v03.png 76 0,0,1,1`. **So the three chainsaw strips are hand-finished:** the importer's output is not the final file, and the greyed prep sheet and the script that greyed it live in the owner's prep folder, not in the repo (the master sheets aren't in the repo either). Re-running the install commands overwrites the three strips without teeth; run the teeth pass again after. It is the same kind of touch-up as v02's pasted X eye. The alternative, one grey only, left the bar's shaded side mauve.
- **Chainsaw rev gets its own 76 px cell** (feet still at (26, 47)): the lifted saw reaches 47 px in front of the feet, and in v02 two frames slid 8 px to stay inside 64, which shook the body. Now no frame slides. The cutting reach (28 px) is unchanged; only `assets.js` and `render.js` read a cell's width, so the touch, top-bar and hitbox code did not change. A sim test pins the table.
- **Victory loops its two sunglasses poses (v02's frames 3, 2, 3, 2 counting from 1: sheet frames s2-r6-07, 06, 07, 06)** instead of starting on a different pose with a big white eye and no sunglasses, which popped every half second. A 4-frame cheer is still wanted.
- **Chainsaw idle holds one pose** (frame 1 four times) instead of alternating two unrelated poses, which read as a twitch. Two breathing frames are still wanted.
- **Land is the fall crouch held, feet at (28, 47), no pasted dust.** v02's two landing frames lunged forward (feet at 17, the head moved 10 px back and 6 px forward within 0.13 s) and carried a pasted dust puff on top of the game's own puff. Until two real landing frames exist, the fall crouch is held for both frames: no lurch, one puff. The land cell is a plain 48/28 cell again.
- **Drawing fix: the code-made placeholder saw is only drawn in the placeholder Sammy's paws.** It used to be painted over real art for the animations that have no chainsaw variant (victory, hurt, jump, fall, land, tail-whip) while he carried the saw. Now real art is drawn as it is (the top bar already shows the saw), and plain idle/run art standing in for a missing chainsaw strip gets nothing extra either. A sim test draws every case against a pretend canvas, and the browser smoke test counts placeholder-saw draws over a full run and every screen (must be 0 with all 13 strips loaded).
- **Known rough spots, left as they are:** the owner's crawl in-betweens are a little chunkier than the sheet poses and carry the tail high, so the tail bobs every other frame at 12 fps; the two fall frames differ a lot (a tuck and a sprawl, flipping at 10 fps); the chain teeth are 1 px dots; a few fur-highlight pixels in the non-chainsaw strips now land on the greys (8-30 px per strip); the hurt frames still lean past the box and are slid back by up to 14 px (hidden by the white flash); the X eyes on play-dead and K.O. still blur to a blob at game size (a hand repaint is listed in `design/OPEN_TASKS.md`); the idle and victory tails still lose about 7 px of curl to the 48 px cell.
- Tests now: 78 game-rule (+1: the placeholder saw), 23 importer (+1: the chainsaw teeth pass keeps the outline), 57 browser (+2: the saw check in the source and built game). `?test=shot&pose=` also takes `victory`, `jump`, `fall`, `land`, `sawidle`, `sawrun` and `sawrev`.

**Step 1 done: the enemy type table (Proposed, reversible; no change in play).** Before, two types were handled by "is it a vendor?" checks in 13 places, and anything unknown was quietly played as a jogger. Now:
- **One row per type in `config.js`:** the `jogger` and `vendor` blocks carry their behaviour next to their numbers: `brain` ('walker' = patrol, chase, lunge; 'turret' = stand and throw), `art` (manifest prefix), `start`/`startAnim`, `flinch`, `knockback`, `sawKnockback`, `cartKnock`, `shieldPush`, `hitY` (how high the attack box starts), `possum` ('confused' or 'shrug'), `possumIgnore` (was the shared `possum.ignoreAfter`), `defeatStyle`, `sawStyle`. The gauntlet's `waveType` ('jogger') says what the waves spawn.
- **`sim.js` builds `S.ENEMY_TYPES`** from every config block with a `brain`, and checks each row at load. An unknown type, a missing field, an unknown brain or a start state the brain doesn't have **throws at once** instead of acting as a jogger (the other choice, "unknown types degrade to joggers" from the design, was rejected: a typo in the level would be silent). The jogger's brain is `updateWalker`, the vendor's `updateTurret`; the game loop calls the row's `update`. `flinch: false` is wired for the sweater and the bosses: the hit flashes white for `hitReactTime` and changes nothing else.
- **`render.js` has a `DRAW` table** (one drawer per type; a missing drawer throws) and gag lines keyed by type first, then by style. **`main.js`'s render sweep** draws every state of every type from the table (14 enemy pictures instead of 9) and the gore demo shows every type's whip death and saw death.
- **Hot dogs carry their own size and gravity** from the thrower's block, so a second throwing type needs no code.
- **Proof of "no change in play":** the autopilot's full playthrough, a 40 s run and two runs that take damage were recorded before the change (stats, every enemy's position, state, hp and style, every sound played: `scratchpad/enemies-impl/stats-before.json`) and again after: byte-identical (md5 `5e5daa5fd28d6285ec6476050b20bde3`). The full run: level clear at frame 3136 (51.8 s), 19 zombies down, 3 hot dogs, no hits.

## 2026-10-19: Importer v2

**What was added:** a **character mode** in `game/tools/import-art.mjs` (helpers `art-detect.mjs`, `art-colour.mjs`, `art-character.mjs`, `art-preview.mjs`, `art-cli.mjs`; tests in `game/tests/import-art.test.mjs`, now 21). The owner's image tools give painterly sheets (no pixel grid, hundreds of colours, soft shading): a row of big poses on top and rows of small frames below. A trial with a hippie zombie showed the old one-strip-at-a-time importer gave strips with different palettes, no clean outline, shimmering frames, acid-green skin that collides with the reserved ichor colour, loose sparks that pushed frames out of the cell, and needed hand-written helper scripts. Everything old still works and is byte-for-byte unchanged (Sammy's run strip is still identical; character mode never touches the old path).

**How it is used:** `--detect` finds every small frame (connected pieces on the flat backdrop; little detached bits join the nearest body; touching frames are cut at their narrow place; frames sorted into rows and into animation groups, using the big poses on top as the guide, or `--groups 4,3,2,3,3`), writes `detect-overlay.png` and a draft `map.json`; `--character map.json` builds every strip in one run and writes `CHARACTER-REPORT.md`, a contact sheet, a palette swatch and before/after pictures. Never writes into `assets/` unless `--install`; never overwrites without `--force`.

**Proposed choices (not approved, all reversible):**
- **One scale, one anchor, one palette per character.** The scale makes a standing walk/idle figure 93% of the cell height (45 px in a 48 px cell; about 90 px in a 96 px cell). A pose that is up to 15% too big for its cell is shrunk on its own; a bigger one lowers the scale for everything (and says so). Each frame stands on the ground line of its sheet row; the feet column is the middle of the legs and hips; a wide pose is slid sideways just enough to fit. The palette is one weighted k-means over all frames (15 colours at most, outline colour included), snapped to the 9-bit grid.
- **Clean-up before reducing colours:** saturation boost 1.55, contrast 1.2, a small lift, then an automatic brightness stretch (night parks are dark; tuned on the five real sheets), a median filter sized from the shrink factor, a 3 x 3 majority clean-up at full size. Shrinking uses an **area vote** (the most common palette colour in each pixel's patch, dark pixels count extra so thin dark lines survive); the plain area average is `--downscale average` (it blends neighbouring shades into muddy in-between colours, so it is not the default).
- **Outline:** a 1 px outline on transparent pixels next to the figure, in the darkest palette colour (or `--outline "#rrggbb"`, or `off`); the figure is kept 1 px inside the cell so it never clips. The outline is never a gore colour.
- **Gore colours:** by default acid yellow-green (hue 62-118, saturated) becomes a desaturated grey-teal and dark saturated blood red becomes rust, before the palette is made, and the finished palette is checked against the six reserved colours. `--allow-blood`, `--allow-ichor`, `--no-avoid-gore-colours`; a slot in `map.json` can have its own palette group with `"gore": "all"` (for example the boss's acid breath). Plain yellow is left alone.
- **Shimmer:** frames of one strip are filtered identically; `--stabilize` (on by default) keeps a pixel the same colour as the frame before when the picture only changed by noise; the report shows how many still pixels flip colour.
- **Loose particles:** a detached bit that falls outside the cell (or too far above the body with `--trim-particles N`) is removed, so defeat frames stay inside the cell; tiny leftovers are removed after shrinking (`--despeckle`). Attached slime stays. `--keep-effects` keeps everything.
- **New enemy types need no manifest edit:** `--prefix NAME`, `--size WxH` (up to 192 x 192) or `"cell"` in the map; slots in the manifest keep their exact size and file name. Portrait/effect slots (`feet: false`) are fitted to their own cell; `--grid-split 10x2` cuts portrait grids.
- **Not covered:** JPG/WebP still need converting; the detector is tuned on the five real sheets (it can still need `--groups` or a hand edit of `map.json`: the sweater sheet needed `--groups 4,5,2,4,3`); there is no automatic way to tell which animation is which beyond the usual order (walk, attack, hit, defeat, idle).

## 2026-10-19: Art import tool

**What was added:** `game/tools/import-art.mjs` (plus `decodeAnyPng` in `game/tools/png.mjs`, tests in `game/tests/import-art.test.mjs`, and the prompt kit `design/ART_PROMPTS.md`). The owner makes art in other tools (ChatGPT, Codex and so on) and brings it here; those pictures are blurry, have hundreds of colours, a solid or checkerboard background and the wrong size. The importer turns one into an exact game strip, a 4x preview and a plain-language report with a verdict (READY, READY WITH WARNINGS, REDO). It never writes into `assets/` unless asked with `--install`, and never overwrites without `--force`. The game itself is unchanged.

**Proposed choices (not approved, all reversible):**
- **PNG only as input.** JPG and WebP would need a decoder with real code behind it (no packages allowed), and JPG's blocky noise is bad for pixel art anyway. The tool says how to convert on a Mac: `sips -s format png "in.jpg" --out out.png`. It does read 1/2/4/8/16-bit greyscale, RGB, palette (with transparency), grey+alpha and RGBA PNGs, including interlaced ones.
- **Median-cut colour reduction** (not k-means): it needs no starting guesses, gives the same answer every run, and is easy to explain. Colours are first snapped to the 9-bit grid; if more than 15 remain, the darkest common colour is reserved as the outline, and the rest are cut into 14 groups by popularity-weighted median.
- **Default scale rule:** a character slot is 48 px tall, so the tallest frame is scaled to **40 px** (ears included); smaller cells (16 px) get the cell height minus 2. The tool **never enlarges** unless asked (`--height`), and a poor-quality tiny picture gets a warning. If the picture is crisp pixel art that was enlarged by a whole number, the real pixel size is found first and used as is when it already fits. All frames of a strip share one scale; `--scale K` copies the scale of another strip so a character's animations match.
- **Placement rule:** feet on the bottom row (47 for 48-high cells), horizontally centred on the slot's feet column using the lower 12% of the figure; frames whose feet are within about 1 px of the others are snapped to the same ground line, higher ones (jumps, falls) are kept. Things that float (pickups) are centred.
- **Never clips:** a pose too wide for its cell stops the import with advice; `--shrink` scales everything down to fit instead.
- **Already-correct strips** (right size, see-through) keep their layout and are only tidied; Sammy's approved run strip comes out pixel for pixel the same (a test checks this).
- **Not covered yet:** background bands (sky, skyline, treeline) that repeat sideways; JPG/WebP; telling which way a figure faces (the prompt kit asks for "facing right"; the preview is for checking it).
